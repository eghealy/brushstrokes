Decision-making

In Ted Chiang’s New Yorker op-ed “Why A.I. isn’t going to make art,” he argues that a 10,000-word short story is created by roughly 10,000 decisions. Likewise, a painting is created by thousands of brushstrokes, each essential to the final work of art. The choices made at every level of the creative process — while plotting, coming up with a title, laboring over the opening sentence, or rendering a new setting — combine to create a unique work of art. A piece created (or heavily influenced) by artificial intelligence glosses over all of the minute decisions and relies on the A.I.’s inherently derivative reasoning to fill in the contours of the project. The result may be readable, or parseable, or lukewarmly pleasing. But it cannot be art.

“Why A.I. isn't going to make art" was published on August 31, 2025. Much has changed since then, but I believe that its central thesis still holds. At the very least, for my own writing practice, I choose to believe that decision-making is essential to creating. To use generative A.I. during the creative process is to eliminate the decision-making that defines art from decoration.

This project, Brushstrokes, creates a visual record of the painstaking decisions essential to creative writing.

Use it as a tool to understand your creative process: see where your story was revised over and over. Which sentences were instinctual, easy, painless. Considered before the words were recorded, like Jack Kerouac’s premeditated scroll rolling through his typewriter. Understand, too, where sentences were revised and revised and revised and revised — where you struggled the most, and, hopefully, were rewarded for your efforts.

I hope that Brushstrokes will encourage writers to embrace the choice-making essential to creating art, no matter how tortured those choices may be. For myself, Brushstrokes serves as a reminder that every word I write is worthy of revision.

Nuance

This website is not a work of art. It is a resource; a demonstration; a way for writers to look at their own work and creative process in a new way. To evaluate their process against an ideal. I created Brushstrokes for myself, as a reminder that each word I write is worthy of revision. I aspire to treat every sentence that crosses my pen with careful consideration.

To create this tool, I used Claude Code. While I have an interest in creative coding and have dabbled in website design, I could never have made this project on my own.

I used A.I. to build the site; to create the javascript app that translates revision history into colors. However, the idea for Brushstrokes, the idea for each feature, each color palette, the site copy – all was written by a human, E.G. Healy.

This is a delicate balance. Using A.I. as a teaching resource, but drawing the line at using A.I. to write prose, is perhaps a little counterintuitive. But I use spellcheck every day – in fact, I appreciate it greatly – even though it is not my own invention.

I do NOT use A.I. to create art. I do not condone the use of A.I. to create art. I have never used A.I. to write creatively, and I am always happy to share the many, many, many drafts that scaffold a finished short story. My first drafts are always handwritten, in my own barely-legible scrawl.

Privacy

A.I. helped create this tool. It cannot read any writing uploaded to Brushstrokes.

I used Claude to write some of the code running this page — the HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. That's the full extent of A.I.'s involvement. Writing the tool and using the tool are two separate things: no A.I. has seen, or will ever see, any document you connect here.

When you connect a Google Doc, your browser talks directly to Google using your own sign-in — there is no backend server for this site. Brushstrokes has no database and no API to send your writing to. Every calculation, from comparing revisions to painting the heatmap, happens locally in your browser's memory and disappears the moment you close the tab.

The comparisons that power this heatmap come from a plain, deterministic text-diffing algorithm — the same category of tool used by version control systems like Git. It's math, not A.I., and version controls have been around for a while. Your writing is never sent to Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, or any other A.I. model, and it is never used to train one.

Google

If you connect a Google Doc to Brushstrokes, your document already lives on Google's servers. Connecting a doc here uses Google's own sign-in to read data you already have access to, the same as opening the doc yourself. Brushstrokes doesn't add a new party into that relationship, and doesn't keep a copy anywhere else.

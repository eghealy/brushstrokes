Art and decisionmaking

In Ted Chiang’s New Yorker op-ed “Why A.I. isn’t going to make art,” he argues that a 10,000-word short story is created by roughly 10,000 decisions. Likewise, a painting is created by thousands of brushstrokes, each essential to the final work of art. The choices made at every level of the creative process — while plotting, coming up with a title, laboring over the opening sentence, or rendering a new setting — combine to create a unique work of art. A piece created (or heavily influenced) by artificial intelligence glosses over all of the minute decisions and relies on the AI’s inherently derivative reasoning to fill in the contours of the project. The result may be readable, or parseable, or lukewarmly pleasing. But it cannot be art.

“Why AI isn't going to make art" was published on August 31, 2025. Much has changed since then, but I believe that its central thesis still holds. At the very least, for my own writing practice, I choose to believe that decision-making is essential to creating. To use generative AI during the creative process is to eliminate the decision-making that defines art from decoration.

This project, Brushstrokes, creates a visual record of the painstaking decisions essential to creative writing.

Use it as a tool to understand your creative process: see where your story was revised over and over. Which sentences were instinctual, easy, painless. Considered before the words were recorded, like Jack Kerouac’s premeditated scroll rolling through his typewriter. Understand, too, where sentences were revised and revised and revised and revised — where you struggled the most, and, hopefully, were rewarded for your efforts.

I hope that Brushstrokes will encourage writers to embrace the choice-making essential to creating art, no matter how tortured those choices may be. For myself, Brushstrokes serves as a reminder that every word I write is worthy of revision.

How this was built, and how your privacy works

AI helped write this tool. It never reads your writing.

Claude, an AI coding assistant, was used to help write the code running this page — the HTML, CSS, and JavaScript you're looking at right now. That's the full extent of AI's involvement. Writing the tool and using the tool are two separate things: no AI has seen, or will ever see, any document you connect here.

Everything runs in your browser. Nothing is uploaded anywhere.

When you connect a Google Doc, your browser talks directly to Google using your own sign-in — there is no backend server for this site. Brushstrokes has no database and no API to send your writing to, even if it wanted to. Every calculation, from comparing revisions to painting the heatmap, happens locally in your browser's memory and disappears the moment you close the tab.

No AI ever reads or trains on your document.

The comparisons that power this heatmap come from a plain, deterministic text-diffing algorithm — the same category of tool used by version control systems like Git. It's math, not AI. Your writing is never sent to Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, or any other AI model, and it is never used to train one.

What Google can still see

Your document already lives on Google's servers, because that's where Google Docs stores it — this tool doesn't change that. Connecting a doc here uses Google's own sign-in to read data you already have access to, the same as opening the doc yourself. Brushstrokes doesn't add a new party into that relationship, and doesn't keep a copy anywhere else.
